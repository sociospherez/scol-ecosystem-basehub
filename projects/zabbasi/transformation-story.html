Context & Intent

This roadmap is the pattern I use when I am brought in to fix, stabilise or evolve complex digital estates: ERP programmes under pressure, fragmented data landscapes, and organisations that need visibility, control and momentum without another 18-month strategy deck.

It is not a rigid methodology. It is a sequence of conversations, decisions and delivery moves that I adapt to each organisation’s culture, risk appetite and maturity.

The Journey in 8 Phases
Phase 0 – Trigger & Context
Why now?
Every engagement starts with a disruption point: a failing ERP go-live, board concern about data quality, regulatory pressure or a plateau in growth. I clarify the real business threat and align sponsors on what success must look like beyond “making the system work”.
Phase 1 – Rapid Assessment & Insight
What is really happening?
I run a focused assessment across architecture, process, data and governance. The goal is not a long report but sharp insight: where risk concentrates, which dependencies are fragile, and where a few targeted wins can quickly rebuild trust in the transformation.
Phase 2 – Roadmap & Governance Frame
How do we move with control?
Using those insights, I shape a pragmatic roadmap: stabilise first, then optimise, then innovate. I define decision rhythms, ownership, KPIs and escalation paths so leadership stays informed without being dragged into daily firefighting.
Phase 3 – ERP & Platform Stabilisation
Make it predictable
I focus on making core platforms—SAP, Business Central or adjacent systems—reliable and boring. That includes cleaning technical-debt hotspots, tightening integrations, standardising environments and aligning vendors on a single definition of “done”. Stability becomes the new normal.
Phase 4 – High-Tech Enablement
Unlock capability
With a stable core, we unlock the higher-value capabilities: remote and high-performance computing, XR for design and review, and workflow tools that actually match how teams work. Technology becomes a quiet enabler rather than a noisy distraction.
Phase 5 – Data, Analytics & Power Platform
See clearly
I introduce or mature self-service analytics and low-code automation, typically with Power BI and Power Platform. Data models and dashboards are built around the questions leaders actually ask, and guardrails ensure citizen development does not compromise security or compliance.
Phase 6 – Automation, AI & Continuous Improvement
Do smarter, not more
Once data flows and processes are visible, we automate the repetitive and selectively augment decision-making with AI where it adds real value. A continuous improvement loop is embedded so each iteration makes the next one easier rather than heavier.
Phase 7 – Operating Model & Innovation Engine
Make it self-sustaining
Finally, we formalise the operating model around the transformed landscape: roles, rituals, metrics and innovation channels. The organisation is left with a living system—governed, measurable and capable of evolving with strategy, not against it.
Where the research and data work fit in: at every phase, I use structured data—architecture maps, usage telemetry, financials, operational metrics—to challenge assumptions and guide decisions. The research you see in my case studies is not an academic layer; it is how we decide what to do next, what to stop doing, and what to protect as we scale.
